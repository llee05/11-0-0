# Career-inspired player traits

`dist/traits.js` owns 25 trait definitions and a curated set of 115 career profiles, keyed by the catalogue's canonical person identity. Each profile includes its source URL and a short rationale, available in the game's Player traits guide. Career traits apply to every edition of the same person, including editions linked through historical identity crosswalks. The modern Tigran L. Petrosian is not the former world champion. Shared fixed abilities are called **career traits**; reserve **signature** for a future ability unique to exactly one player. There are currently no exclusive signature abilities.

The evidence concerns recurring playing preferences and habits, rather than a single famous game or tournament. A career trait highlights one aspect of a player's career; it does not claim that their style was static or limited to that aspect. Some published material is a player's own assessment, and some is a grandmaster's analysis or a FIDE career profile. Nakamura's citation is a print interview: Karsten Müller and Raymund Stolze, *Fighting Chess with Hikaru Nakamura* (Edition Olms, 2012), chapter 6, p. 138, interview by IM Georgios Souleidis in July 2011. Its URL links the publisher record, not the interview text.

The expansion covers every person with a sampled edition rated at least 2750 and many notable players below that level. This is a tested coverage floor, not a rating-based way to invent traits: additions still require evidence. Young players' entries describe their careers so far. Where a source describes development toward a broader style, the rationale retains that qualification. Preparation may reflect recurring analytical work, and an endgame career trait may highlight demonstrated technique without claiming it was the player's only preference.

Source locations include GM Mihail Marin's *Learn from the Legends 2* preface, pp. 7–8 (Keres, Bronstein, Stein, Portisch and Polugaevsky); Tibor Károlyi's Seirawan interviews, pp. 9–11 and 16; Thomas Engqvist's Andersson preface, pp. 12–13; Keith Arkell's *Arkell's Endings* preface, pp. 10–11 (Speelman and Howell); and the official Biel 2007 player booklet, pp. 5–10. Geller's citation is the publisher's *The Nemesis* excerpt hosted by a retailer. Nunn's citation is the publisher description of his own game collection, reproduced by MIT Press Bookstore. Kamsky's interview in *Chess Life*, September 2012, p. 20, and Short's ECF 2025 career profile were verified through indexed primary-source passages; the links point to their full issues, whose complete PDFs could not be fetched during this review. Each in-game entry retains its specific attribution.

**All numerical effects, tradeoffs and simulated match conditions are fictional balance rules.** A negative modifier is not a biographical claim about a player's weakness. Traits never modify a published source rating, catalogue ranking or national roster. There are no simulated chess moves: opening theory, sharp play, quiet maneuvering, technical endings and time pressure are abstract conditions used to activate different abilities.

Unprofiled players have a 25% chance of no trait and otherwise an equal chance of each trait. Those rolls are game data, not claims about the real person. Roll once for each appearance in a draft offer, including its disabled candidates, and retain the chosen player's result through the game. A new offer may roll a different trait for an unprofiled edition. Famous career traits never reroll. Tournament opponents receive their own seeded rolls at the beginning and keep them for the tournament.

The match resolver evaluates both sides' abilities simultaneously. Individual trait Elo bonuses are capped at 80 per side; defensive counters only reduce positive bonuses. Draw changes are percentage-point changes, combined from both players and bounded to keep probabilities valid. They change result variance while preserving the expected score implied by effective Elo and color. Source Elo, fatigue loss and individual game modifiers are displayed separately. Style, form and daily performance stack separately from the bounded individual trait bonus. Both user and opponent players follow the same trait and fatigue rules.

Trait rules have a separate version marker from the SQL catalogue. Saved assignments must include an explicit `null` for no trait; missing or unknown traits and altered career traits are rejected. The expansion uses the marker `career-traits-5`; earlier version-4 saves are backed up before a fresh draft, preserving their old random assignments rather than silently rewriting a tournament. Increment the trait rules marker and deliberately handle save compatibility when changing rules or assignments.

## Player profiles

`dist/player-profile.js` adds a playing style plus stamina and consistency on a 1–5 scale. Famous career traits map to fixed game-archetype presets, shared across their editions. These numerical presets are balance choices, **not sourced measurements of the real players' endurance or reliability**. The existing career evidence supports the career trait/style interpretation only. Other players roll each attribute and style independently on each new offer. All players, including famous players, roll starting form for that run.

Fatigue load is an integer 0–100. Each played round adds `14 − stamina` load, bounded at 100; sitting out removes 30, bounded at zero. Effective Elo loses `max(0, load − 24)`, halved for grinders. Thus a tired player can retain some load after a rest. Substitution and preview alone never recover load. Consecutive-game streaks remain separate informational state. The same rules and complete temporal save validation apply to all 32 teams. Persistent fatigue was introduced in `squad-profiles-2`. The current `squad-profiles-3` removes captain attributes and bonuses; incompatible saves are backed up before a fresh draft.

Reserve planning compares every possible player to rest over up to three remaining rounds. It maximises expected four-board match points using current trait/style matchups, draw probabilities, source Elo, fatigue and form. Unknown future rounds use averaged effects against a 2720 reference mix; current opposing lineups are projected independently of user substitutions, and user recommendations use the visible AI lineup. Future form is held constant when playing and moves toward neutral when resting; actual future outcomes and performance swings are unknown to the planner. Ties favor current strength, then resting the weaker source-rated player, then canonical edition ID. Opponents always use the planner. The user can request its recommendation or apply it automatically through Auto-rotate & finish.

The user acts as captain by selecting players, choosing four starters and managing reserves. Neither side has a captain attribute, a selected captain or a leadership bonus. The read-only Team profiles dialog exposes the remaining attributes for lineup decisions.

Attacking styles favor sharp play and reduce draws; positional styles favor quiet and technical conditions; defensive styles counter attacking styles and increase draws; universal styles give a small constant bonus. These effects stack separately with the existing individual trait.

Form ranges from −2 to +2, worth 12 Elo per level. After a played game, beating the board's expected score by more than 0.15 raises form one level; falling short by more than 0.15 lowers it. Rest moves form one level toward neutral. Consistency controls the daily performance swing: levels 1–5 allow up to ±32, ±24, ±16, ±8 and ±4 Elo respectively. Performance is independently seeded by tournament, round, team and player; previewing or changing boards does not reroll it. Both sides use the same rules.


## Automated drafting

Country-year offer weights double for each 50 Elo above 2600 of a strength measure averaging the strongest eligible player's rating and the eligible top-three mean. Every qualifying snapshot with an eligible player retains positive probability. Quick Draft and Quick Sim choose by a common eleven-round projection: four games followed by one rest, repeating; actual fatigue equations; averaged trait/style matchups; starting form decaying by 0.65 per round; and the exact rounded-uniform consistency distribution. This is a transparent game heuristic, not a prediction of a real player's career or a promise of an optimal tournament squad. Existing picks, trait rolls and offer profiles are retained.


## Conditional balance rules

Career trait assignments and fixed style/stamina/consistency presets stay tied to their existing profiles. Introduced in `career-traits-3` and retained in `career-traits-5`, specialists and counters receive larger bonuses in favourable conditions, up to 80 effective Elo, while the universal trait supplies a constant 8. Sacrificers receive 64 in sharp play, rising to 80 when behind, and retain their technical and draw tradeoffs. Prophylaxis retains its half-strength block and gains a small quiet/technical bonus. Practical players also gain time-pressure and quiet/technical benefits. The defender's bonus rises gradually from 0 to 48 as the pre-trait deficit grows from 20 to 100 Elo; its favourite penalty rises from 0 to 8 across a 60–140 Elo advantage. Half-Elo rounding avoids large threshold jumps. Grinder fatigue recovery rules are unchanged. Every ability description in the game lists its active effects; all 32 teams use the same rules.

These values balance complete profiles and contextual roles. They do not make every ability equal in every situation or make a 2600 player generally stronger than a 2800 player. Known conditions and counters give the manager choices; source ratings, board ordering and source claims remain intact.

The two-year catalogue expansion adds Li Chao (attacking play), Wang Hao (positional play), Pavel Eljanov (endgame technique) and Reuben Fine (all-round precision), bringing the sourced set to 98. Their original sources and qualifications are embedded in `dist/traits.js`. Earlier catalogue/rules saves follow the existing backup flow; completed personal reports remain independently readable.

The pre-1927 Legends extension adds seventeen sourced career profiles, bringing the total to 115 under `career-traits-5`. Coverage includes every newly sampled source edition at least 2750, plus Morphy, Anderssen and Nimzowitsch. Réti’s own contemporary analysis, FIDE museum career portraits, GM/author analysis and documented contemporary assessments support the choices. Fixed profiles highlight one aspect of a career and retain qualifications about changing styles.
