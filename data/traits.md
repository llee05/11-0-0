# Career-inspired player traits

`dist/traits.js` owns 25 trait definitions and a curated set of 94 career profiles, keyed by the catalogue's canonical person identity. Each profile includes its source URL and a short rationale, available in the game's Player traits guide. Career traits apply to every edition of the same person, including editions linked through historical identity crosswalks. The modern Tigran L. Petrosian is not the former world champion. Shared fixed abilities are called **career traits**; reserve **signature** for a future ability unique to exactly one player. There are currently no exclusive signature abilities.

The evidence concerns recurring playing preferences and habits, rather than a single famous game or tournament. A career trait highlights one aspect of a player's career; it does not claim that their style was static or limited to that aspect. Some published material is a player's own assessment, and some is a grandmaster's analysis or a FIDE career profile. Nakamura's citation is a print interview: Karsten Müller and Raymund Stolze, *Fighting Chess with Hikaru Nakamura* (Edition Olms, 2012), chapter 6, p. 138, interview by IM Georgios Souleidis in July 2011. Its URL links the publisher record, not the interview text.

The expansion covers every person with a sampled edition rated at least 2750 and many notable players below that level. This is a tested coverage floor, not a rating-based way to invent traits: additions still require evidence. Young players' entries describe their careers so far. Where a source describes development toward a broader style, the rationale retains that qualification. Preparation may reflect recurring analytical work, and an endgame career trait may highlight demonstrated technique without claiming it was the player's only preference.

Source locations include GM Mihail Marin's *Learn from the Legends 2* preface, pp. 7–8 (Keres, Bronstein, Stein, Portisch and Polugaevsky); Tibor Károlyi's Seirawan interviews, pp. 9–11 and 16; Thomas Engqvist's Andersson preface, pp. 12–13; Keith Arkell's *Arkell's Endings* preface, pp. 10–11 (Speelman and Howell); and the official Biel 2007 player booklet, pp. 5–10. Geller's citation is the publisher's *The Nemesis* excerpt hosted by a retailer. Nunn's citation is the publisher description of his own game collection, reproduced by MIT Press Bookstore. Kamsky's interview in *Chess Life*, September 2012, p. 20, and Short's ECF 2025 career profile were verified through indexed primary-source passages; the links point to their full issues, whose complete PDFs could not be fetched during this review. Each in-game entry retains its specific attribution.

**All numerical effects, tradeoffs and simulated match conditions are fictional balance rules.** A negative modifier is not a biographical claim about a player's weakness. Traits never modify a published source rating, catalogue ranking or national roster. There are no simulated chess moves: opening theory, sharp play, quiet maneuvering, technical endings and time pressure are abstract conditions used to activate different abilities.

Unprofiled players have a 25% chance of no trait and otherwise an equal chance of each trait. Those rolls are game data, not claims about the real person. Roll once for each appearance in a draft offer, including its disabled candidates, and retain the chosen player's result through the game. A new offer may roll a different trait for an unprofiled edition. Famous career traits never reroll. Tournament opponents receive their own seeded rolls at the beginning and keep them for the tournament.

The match resolver evaluates both sides' abilities simultaneously. Individual trait Elo bonuses are capped at 40 per side; defensive counters only reduce positive bonuses. Draw changes are percentage-point changes, combined from both players and bounded to keep probabilities valid. They change result variance while preserving the expected score implied by effective Elo and color. Source Elo, fatigue loss and individual game modifiers are displayed separately. Style, form, captain influence and daily performance stack separately from the bounded individual trait bonus. Both user and opponent players follow the same trait and fatigue rules.

Trait rules have a separate version marker from the SQL catalogue. Saved assignments must include an explicit `null` for no trait; missing or unknown traits and altered career trait traits are rejected. The expansion advances the marker to `career-traits-2`; earlier version-4 saves are backed up before a fresh draft, preserving their old random assignments rather than silently rewriting a tournament. Increment the trait rules marker and deliberately handle save compatibility when changing rules or assignments.

## Player profiles

`dist/player-profile.js` adds a playing style plus stamina, captaincy and consistency on a 1–5 scale. Famous career traits map to fixed game-archetype presets, shared across their editions. These numerical presets are balance choices, **not sourced measurements of the real players' endurance, leadership or reliability**. The existing career evidence supports the career trait/style interpretation only. Other players roll each attribute and style independently on each new offer. All players, including famous players, roll starting form for that run.

Fatigue load is an integer 0–100. Each played round adds `14 − stamina` load, bounded at 100; sitting out removes 30, bounded at zero. Effective Elo loses `max(0, load − 24)`, halved for grinders. Thus a tired player can retain some load after a rest. Substitution and preview alone never recover load. Consecutive-game streaks remain separate informational state. The same rules and complete temporal save validation apply to all 32 teams. These mechanics advance `PROFILE_RULESET` to `squad-profiles-2`, requiring a fresh draft while backing up incompatible saves.

Reserve planning compares every possible player to rest over up to three remaining rounds. It sums projected source Elo minus fatigue plus form and captain influence. Future form is held constant when playing and moves toward neutral when resting; actual future outcomes and performance swings are unknown to the planner. Ties favor current strength, then resting the weaker source-rated player, then canonical edition ID. Opponents always use the planner. The user can request its recommendation or apply it automatically through Auto-rotate & finish.

A playing captain supplies `3 × (captaincy − 1)` Elo to other active players, with half influence when the captain is the reserve. There is one captain per team, and the captain never buffs themself. This remains a fictional playing-captain rule, rather than a model of the administrative responsibilities of a real non-playing Olympiad captain.

Attacking styles favor sharp play and reduce draws; positional styles favor quiet and technical conditions; defensive styles counter attacking styles and increase draws; universal styles give a small constant bonus. These effects stack separately with the existing individual trait.

Form ranges from −2 to +2, worth 12 Elo per level. After a played game, beating the board's expected score by more than 0.15 raises form one level; falling short by more than 0.15 lowers it. Rest moves form one level toward neutral. Consistency controls the daily performance swing: levels 1–5 allow up to ±32, ±24, ±16, ±8 and ±4 Elo respectively. Performance is independently seeded by tournament, round, team and player; previewing or changing captain/board does not reroll it. Both sides use the same rules.
