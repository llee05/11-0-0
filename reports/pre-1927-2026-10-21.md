# Players before 1927 · 21 October 2026

Recommendation: extend country-year offers selectively into the 1920s first, then pilot a nineteenth-century source with wider coverage. **Five-player depth is a real evidence constraint; uniformly low star ratings are not.** No pre-1927 players or speculative national rosters have been imported by this investigation.

These are different methods and periods: an Edo annual estimate is not a January Chessmetrics observation or an official rating. Their discrepancies are a reason to choose and document a source policy, rather than select whichever rating is highest. The January 1858 Chessmetrics table does not list Morphy; that omission is not evidence that he was weak. These sampled months do not establish the maximum available coverage for every earlier year.

[Chessmetrics describes](http://www.chessmetrics.com/cm/CM2/SourceData.asp) using match/tournament scores even when moves are unavailable, and identifies gaps in its historical inputs. Extending a rating catalogue and building the move-analysis mode therefore have different data requirements.

## Do countries need five strong players?

The current rule needs **five real, rated, distinct players with supported period affiliations, with at least one source rating ≥2600**. The other four may be below 2600. The user chooses one player from each country-year offer; drafting Morphy would not force the other four players from his country into the squad. Country depth still affects offer weighting, because the leader and top-three mean determine how often the offer appears.

There are plausible nineteenth-century pools worth auditing. For example, the [1857 American Congress book](https://books.google.com/books?id=_TtQAQAAIAAJ), compiled by organiser Daniel Willard Fiske, is a contemporary starting point for investigating its participants. Five of those participants have the following observations in the [Edo 1857 table](http://www.edochess.ca/years/y1857.html):

| Candidate | Edo source ID | Annual estimate |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Paul Morphy | p23 | 2805 |
| Louis Paulsen | p28 | 2627 |
| Theodor Lichtenhein | p30 | 2499 |
| Charles Stanley | p24 | 2376 |
| Frederic Perrin | p115 | 2304 |

This demonstrates a rated five-person candidate pool, **not an audited USA top five**. Congress attendance, residence and later nationality do not by themselves determine the game's sporting affiliation. Every higher-rated possible member, including nonparticipants, still needs examination. Paulsen's later German career must not simply be projected back onto an earlier American period. Other candidate countries and years need the same work; this study does not count verified country offers.

If a country genuinely has fewer than five supported entries, keep that country-year unavailable. Filling it with invented ratings, duplicate people or players borrowed from neighbouring years would change the meaning of a snapshot.

## Edo is a useful second-source pilot

[Edo's methodology](http://www.edochess.ca/Edo.explanation.html) connects annual player estimates across neighbouring years and uses earlier and later results. It publishes rating deviations and includes years with no recorded games; an annual value should not be presented as a rating known at that year's start. Some inputs are casual or handicap results with modelled adjustments. The lists also distinguish Anderssen's formal and offhand play. These distinctions matter for source labelling and person uniqueness.

The inspected 1858 table has both Anderssen `p13` and “Anderssen, Adolf (offhand)” `p478`. An importer must link them to one person and select an explicit supported edition policy, preventing two copies in a draft or opposing field. Retain the original observations and deviations even when an entry is excluded from gameplay. A source deviation measures uncertainty in that source's estimate; it should not silently become the game's daily consistency statistic.

The site's [current year index](http://www.edochess.ca/Edo.years.html) reaches 1948. Its [homepage](http://www.edochess.ca/index.html) identifies Rod Edwards and a 17 July 2026 update. Pinning exact versions matters: the sample is evidence from that retrieved release, not a permanent coverage guarantee. Before a production import, establish reuse terms and attribution and check identities against existing Chessmetrics/FIDE people. Extra source records can help establish a fifth player without making the estimates equivalent to modern Elo.

## Suggested implementation order

1. **1926 and 1924 pilot:** use the existing Chessmetrics source family and start with countries whose earlier affiliations can be supported. Re-audit at the actual date; do not copy 1927 nationality decisions backwards through emigration or border changes.
2. **One nineteenth-century pilot:** audit an Edo year such as 1857, preserving annual period semantics, source deviations and canonical identities. Establish the full source-ranked national top five before publishing. Keep the source eligibility cutoff unchanged for the initial pilot.
3. **Support bounded historical lists deliberately:** today's publication builder requires exactly 100 Chessmetrics rows. A shorter source could support a national top five only when its fifth player is inside the published boundary and every higher-ranked possible member is audited. Store coverage explicitly; never pad a short list or compute a top-100 statistic from it. This investigation's sparse parser is research-only; the publication guard remains strict.
4. **Evaluate discoverability and balance:** measure actual draft frequency and squad outcomes with fixed rosters/seeds. Lower supporting ratings need not stop a star being drafted, but they can make an offer rare. Any dedicated historical-offer weighting or adjusted-mode eligibility would be an explicit versioned game rule, separate from the source data. Do not apply the top-100 era-mapping prototype blindly to sparse early lists.

For isolated stars whose country cannot supply five supported players, an optional **Legends offer** could show five distinct, sourced players across countries or periods, each retaining their actual edition and affiliation qualifications. That would be a new offer type with its own draft/save rules, rather than a national snapshot. It preserves the existing four-board-plus-reserve squad structure without inventing historical teammates. No such offer type is implemented here.

The [two proposed rating modes](era-ratings-2026-10-21.md) remain separate: a fictional era-balance mode can target enjoyable competition; a move-based strength estimate needs verified game scores and calibrated analysis. Neither can recover missing country affiliations or guarantee that sparse historical records represent a player's full career.

## Reproduction and checks

Download the exact URLs in `pre-1927-sources-2026-10-21.json` to an ignored directory using the manifest's filenames. HTTP was required for the original Chessmetrics/Edo pages during this investigation; the script checks their SHA256 before parsing and rejects changed responses or wrong period titles. It performs no downloads or catalogue/database writes itself:

```bash
python -B scripts/research-early-history.py --source-dir .data/early-history-research --output /tmp/pre-1927.json
node --test --test-isolation=none tests/early-history.test.mjs
```

The generated report includes parser/script hashes, raw-source hashes, counts, source deviations and selected examples. Checks cover all twelve pinned tables, ordered unique source IDs/ranks, exact source titles and unchanged strict top-100 parsing. All 23 existing 100-row observation tables also pass strict parsing; eleven cached original pages reproduce their complete checked-in JSON. The final application suite passes all 73 tests, and syntax/whitespace checks pass. Sparse, empty and alternate-entry research fixtures cannot enter the catalogue through this tool. Source IDs are not assumed to establish unique people or national affiliations.
